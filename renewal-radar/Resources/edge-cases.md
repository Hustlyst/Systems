# Renewal Radar edge cases

These are the situations where a monthly radar gives a wrong picture, and what to do in each.

## A multi-year contract in the middle of its term

There is no renewal date for a year or more, so the account never enters the window, and risk builds up unseen. Give every multi-year account one read a year, on the anniversary of its start date, even when it is outside the window. Flag any change of decider at once, whatever the date.

## Consumption or usage pricing

There may be no renewal event at all. The real deadline is the date after which the customer cannot use up what they committed to. Treat that date as the opt-out date. A burn rate falling behind the commitment pace is the signal to watch.

## Auto-renewing contracts

An auto-renew makes renewal look certain until the notice date. Treat the notice date as a real decision point, and read the account before it as you would any other.

## An account still being set up

An account whose renewal arrives before it has gone live is a churn risk by default. It has no value story yet. Read it as normal, and add a task to agree what "live" means with the customer and by when.

## A book you have just taken over

There is no history and no previous radar. Run a first read on every account in the window. Treat any account where you cannot reach the previous owner's notes as "decider unmet" until you have met them.

## A very large book

With more than about 40 accounts in the window, a full read on every account takes too long. Read the 10 largest accounts in full. For the rest, scan for the tier 1 and tier 2 signals in `renewal-risk` only, and give any account that shows one a full read.

## An acquisition on either side

When the customer is acquired, the buyer's standard tools usually win, on the buyer's timeline. Read the account as at risk from the day the deal is announced. When the customer buys another company, treat it as a chance to expand and a risk of a competitive review at the same time.

## Very little material on an account

When an account has no notes, no recent emails and no usage data, the Radar cannot make a read. List it under "Not checked". Quiet accounts are often the riskiest, so the task is to get a conversation on the calendar this month.
