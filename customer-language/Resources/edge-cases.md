# Customer Language edge cases

These are the situations where the standard method gives a weak or misleading bank, and what to do in each.

## Fewer than 30 sources

The patterns will be thin, and one loud customer can look like a trend. Build the bank anyway, but mark every pattern below five sources as provisional. Add a task to gather more: five short calls asking recent customers what made them start looking is often enough.

## A new product with no customers yet

Use competitors' reviews, forum threads and community posts about the problem. Mark the whole bank as **borrowed**, because these are words about other products. Replace borrowed patterns with your own customers' words as soon as you have them.

## More than one kind of buyer

A clinic manager and a practice owner use different words for the same problem. Keep a separate bank for each segment, and never mix their patterns in one piece of copy. When you cannot tell which segment a source came from, leave it out of both.

## Long sales cycles with several buyers

In B2B deals the person who feels the pain is often not the person who signs. Label each quote with the speaker's role. Copy for the user leads with pain and result. Copy for the person who signs leads with result and alternatives, in money and risk terms.

## Reviews written for a reward

Reviews collected with a discount or a gift card skew positive and generic. Count them, but give them half the weight of other sources when deciding what is a pattern.

## Sources in another language

Translate them, keep the original next to the translation, and mark the quote as translated. Never use a translated phrase in a headline without checking it with a native speaker from that market.

## Regulated industries

In health, finance and similar fields, a customer's words can make a claim your company is not allowed to make, such as a promise of results. Mark any such quote as **for understanding only**. Rewrite the idea within what your legal team allows.

## A pattern that conflicts with your positioning

Sometimes customers clearly value something your company does not want to lead with, such as price when you sell on quality. Report it plainly. Whether to follow the customers or the strategy is a business decision, so the skill shows the evidence and does not choose.
