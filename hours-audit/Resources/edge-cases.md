# Hours Audit edge cases

These are the situations where an hours audit gives a misleading picture, and what to do in each.

## Requests spread across several channels

Requests often arrive by email, chat, a form and in person. Auditing one channel can make that channel's types look like the whole picture. Export every channel you can for the same month. For requests that arrive in person, ask the team to keep a simple tally for two weeks and add it.

## A month that was not typical

Year-end, a product launch or a policy change can flood one type. Note the event in the report. Compare the type with the same month last year if you can, and do not build a large fix on one unusual month.

## Very short or vague requests

Messages such as "any update?" or "see attached" cannot be labelled reliably. Mark them low confidence, check a sample by hand, and if there are many, treat the vague request itself as a finding. It often means people have no other way to check status.

## Personal or sensitive requests

Requests about pay, health or personal matters should not go into an AI tool unless the company allows it. Remove them before labelling, count them separately, and say in the report that they were left out.

## Thousands of requests

Label a random sample of a few hundred instead of everything, and scale the counts up in the spreadsheet. Say in the report that it is a sample and how large. Recheck the top three types against the full export.

## The team disagrees on minutes

Use the middle estimate and show the range. If the ranking of the top three would change with a different estimate, time ten real examples of each before choosing a fix.

## A fix that saves hours for one team and adds them to another

Self-serve and automation sometimes move work instead of removing it. Before recommending a fix, name who does the work afterwards. Count a fix as working only when the total hours fall.

## Nobody owns the fix

A fix with no owner does not happen. If no one can be named, the fix waits, and the report says so. The ownership gap is often the real root cause.
